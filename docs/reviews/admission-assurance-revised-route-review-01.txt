## 3. Findings — analytical judgment

### 1. HIGH — separating clarification regressions can pass unnoticed by the non-regression condition

Sources: `T:78–89`, `T:97–111`, `T:130–136`; `MK/methods/M2-test-drive-docs-by-execution.md:48–56`.

The decision axis is described as serving “decision non-regression” at `T:83`. However, condition 3 prohibits regression only on controls and on nonseparating probes. It does not cover a **separating clarification**.

A falsifying mathematical witness, not an executed test:

| One separating clarification | Predecessor | Candidate | No document |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative decision scores | `(1,1,1)` | `(1,1,0)` | `(0,0,0)` |
| Conservative grounded scores | `(2,2,2)` | `(2,2,1)` | Irrelevant to favorable evidence |

All nine answers can be present, eligible and faithfully judged. The candidate’s third answer can be safely incomplete rather than wrong or unsafe, with no substantive judge defect or unresolved document ambiguity.

This case:

- Satisfies the candidate floor: two grounded twos, no grounded zero.
- Separates from no document by more than the stated margin.
- Loses both correctness and grounded usability against the predecessor.
- Escapes condition 3 because it is neither a control nor nonseparating.

A different separating clarification can improve and satisfy condition 4; all other probes can tie, satisfy coverage and meet their floors. The comparison can therefore PASS despite the first loss.

This is a prospective acceptance-rule issue, not unknown evaluator behavior. The phrase “Gains on other probes cannot compensate” does not close the hole because its operative condition omits this category. It also leaves M2’s warning that gains must not hide damage unresolved.

**Required now:** explicitly resolve how material regressions on separating clarification probes affect the gate, consistently across both axes and the claimed non-compensation rule. If tolerating such losses is intentional, that is a substantive acceptance choice requiring explicit prospective justification and authority—not an implementation detail for the packet author to infer.

### 5. MEDIUM — no-document separation is correctly framed, but supports only the stated descriptive claim

The proposed rule meets M2’s principal separation requirements at design level:

- Same questions and decision criteria for old, new and no-document arms.
- Either document arm may establish separation, in either direction.
- Integer totals implement the preregistered one-third margin.
- Controls receive the same separation test.
- Nonseparating probes supply no favorable evidence but retain adverse accounting.
- Complete presence of all nine units is required for favorable separation.
