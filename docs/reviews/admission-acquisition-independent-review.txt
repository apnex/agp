# Independent admission acquisition/lifecycle review

**Technical verdict: FAIL — revise before implementation.**  
**Scoped M7 verdict: FAIL — revise before implementation.**

The proposed lifecycle and custody model is useful for its declared slice, but its redemption protocol does not reconcile credential-expiry validation with the later clock instant it designates as redemption. This is a contract-level timing gap, not a complaint that proposed APIs have not yet been implemented.

## Findings

### High priority, blocking: expiry validation and the declared redemption instant are different events

The candidate's [redemption sequence](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:355), lines 355–369, requires:

1. Issuer validation of proof, expiry and allocation generation.
2. A subsequent `recordAdmissionRedemption()` call that captures the shared clock instant.
3. Installation of the prepared spent record and lifecycle registration.

Lines 367–369 explicitly make the recorded monotonic instant the redemption anchor adopted by the issuer and require all fallible issuer validation to precede it.

The declared [redemption API](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:261), lines 261–273, accepts only `redemptionId`. Its successful result exposes `RedeemedAdmissionContext`, whose [declared fields](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:105), lines 105–108, are the reference and lifecycle observation. The node samples the clock and retains the absolute bound privately at [line 400](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:400). Neither authoritative credential expiry nor the sampled redemption instant crosses this interface.

A permitted logical ordering is:

- The issuer checks expiry at `tCheck`.
- The key expires.
- The node samples `tRecord`, with the acquisition still otherwise active.
- Recording succeeds and the issuer installs its prepared state.

This requires no asynchronous gap, competing writer or reentrant callback. A monotonic clock can advance during bounded synchronous work. Holding serialization protects state from competing writers; it does not stop time. Sharing the same clock object removes clock-domain disagreement; it does not make two observations simultaneous.

If redemption is `tRecord`, as the candidate states, the key can be expired at redemption. If redemption is instead the earlier validity check, the opening deadline sampled later is not anchored to that earlier event, and the exact lifetime-plus-establishment bound has changed.

This conflicts with:

- Candidate [lines 390–392](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:390): credential expiry limits successful redemption.
- Zorg's [expiry-at-redemption requirement](/home/apnex/taceng/zorg/upstream/agp-review-response-reply.md:49).
- The issuer's [lifetime bound](/home/apnex/taceng/credential-issuer/CONTRACT.md:73), with zero clock tolerance/difference in the first same-process composition.

The generic `expired` recording result does not resolve this: the node knows its acquisition/opening bounds, but the declared request supplies no issuer credential-expiry boundary. A later issuer check would also need an explicitly revised commit/refusal protocol; the present text places fallible validation before recording.

**Consequence:** the proposal cannot establish its claimed redemption-validity and lifetime-plus-establishment guarantee for every permitted execution. An implementer must invent an additional temporal protocol or contradict the stated ordering.

**Minimum repair and acceptance criteria:**

- Declare one exact linearization instant against which credential validity is determined and from which the inherited opening bound is derived.
- Declare the information and authority crossing the issuer/node seam that makes both obligations possible.
- Preserve issuer ownership of credential policy, the prohibition on issuer callbacks inside the node coordinator, and rejection of unchecked clock-domain timestamps.
- Specify the normal refusal/disposition when expiry is crossed before successful registration, including the disposition of any already-recorded lifecycle fact.
- Preserve complete issuer registration before publication, irreversibility of genuinely spent authority, and inability of late work to revive a terminated use.

A statement that the operation is “synchronous” or “atomic” is insufficient unless the exact clock/validity event is defined.

### Medium priority, nonblocking clarification: ordinary stop versus frozen numerical-terminal accounting

Candidate [lines 513–514](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:513) say that on node stop/failure terminal authority is installed before “freezing terminal operations state.”

B53 distinguishes two cases:

- Ordinary stopped instances can continue reporting cleanup progress through resource gauges and revisions.
- Numerical operational-domain exhaustion freezes terminal values, revision and capture time.

See [B53 lines 391–403](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/reviews/admission-lifetime-correction-r4.md:391) and [479–481](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/reviews/admission-lifetime-correction-r4.md:479).

The candidate's [line 531](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:531) more specifically discusses node-terminal replacement and final capture. A compatible reading is therefore available: B53's specific distinction continues to govern, and lines 513–514 refer to the relevant terminal replacement case.

I adopt that conservative reading for this review. This is not a second independently established blocker.

**Risk:** an implementation following the broader wording could freeze ordinary-stop resource accounting prematurely, leaving stale retained-resource counts or accidentally changing B53 semantics.

**Acceptance criterion:** explicitly distinguish ordinary authority revocation at stop from numerical-terminal operational freezing. If a different accounting policy is intended, record it as a deliberate compatibility change. Terminal lifecycle authority must never be mistaken for proof of physical resource release.

### Relevant layers and tensions

- **Issuer policy versus node temporal authority:** keeping credentials outside AGP is correct, but the owner of validity must be able to validate the exact event used by the deadline owner.
- **Synchronous issuer registration versus actor serialization:** a queued actor cannot be awaited while issuer serialization is held. B53's bounded synchronous entry must preserve one mutation authority without external callbacks.
- **Logical safety versus physical release:** terminal publication authority permits some lease reasoning; it does not prove sockets, promises or descendant work have disappeared.
- **Bounded retention versus availability:** unknown state and late work consume capacity. Refusing new work is consistent with safety; evicting live state to regain capacity is not.
- **Consumer-specific usefulness versus wider deployment claims:** the first same-process composition can satisfy a real D62 need without claiming remote clocks, browser profiles or production-scale assurance.

## Implementation guardrails and required closeout

After the blocking contract is repaired and independently re-reviewed, subsequent implementation work should retain these gates:

1. **Redemption boundary:** independently check that credential validity and the establishment deadline use the declared single event, including passage of time between preparatory validation and recording, exact equality, refusal, and incomplete registration. No such runtime validation was performed here.
2. **Custody transition coverage:** enumerate each binding-owned, sealed, delivered, queued, adopted and terminal state. Verify one custodian, one disposition, no ownership restoration by replay, and cleanup after node stop.
3. **Resource conservation:** account separately for pending/live lifecycle cells, acquisition work, provider work and native carrier resources. Late or terminal-but-unsettled work must remain charged; finish and terminal observation must not become early refunds.
4. **Coordinator integration:** inventory every relevant B53 entry and publication path. Normalize external properties outside the commit, preflight complete ledger/projection changes, prohibit nested mutation and callbacks, and revoke permits when post-commit arming fails.
5. **Deadline arithmetic and clock behavior:** verify finite, representable absolute bounds; monotonicity and non-reentrancy assumptions; equality expiry; no phase reset; and no revival by late timer/provider/transport completion.
6. **Exact-controller admission and termination:** verify identity, collision ownership, B51 association and admitted lifecycle history install together. Denial, stale completion, collision loss, stop and termination intent must not leave partial admitted authority.
7. **Compatibility:** verify public exports, types, configuration/schema validation, ordinary transport behavior, fail-closed unsupported combinations and old-runtime behavior. Author compile sketches do not establish package compatibility.
8. **Consumer closeout:** obtain explicit agreement on the repaired redemption event, lifecycle registration obligation, conservative grace-start rule and unknown-retains semantics. Preserve all four D62 release conditions and issuer serialization against issuance, redemption and renewal.
9. **Finding lifecycle:** retain this report against its measured input. Review a new hash after revision, explicitly resolve the blocker and clarification, and later attach authorized validation evidence. Do not edit the original finding into an apparent historical PASS.

Fresh-credential-per-dial, explicit-refusal rearm, browser crypto/carriage, actual scale limits and deployment assurance remain separate gates. This review does not silently authorize or complete them.
