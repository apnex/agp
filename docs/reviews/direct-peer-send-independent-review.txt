## 3. Technical assessment

The mechanism is appropriate to the stated scope.

It preserves named-endpoint routing, makes the stricter policy opt-in, permits legitimate reconnection before admission, and avoids adding request/reply coordination or credential issuance to AGP.
The explicit method also provides a fail-closed support boundary that an optional property on the existing permissive API would not provide by itself.

The following failure hypotheses were challenged:

| Failure hypothesis | Assessment against the draft |
|---|---|
| An ordinary pin can still admit the reply onto another peer's channel | Addressed by direct-candidate filtering before selection and explicit refusal without ordinary-send fallback, lines 292-307 |
| A later allocation of the same node and principal receives an older reply | Addressed by per-allocation `instanceBinding`, lines 99-113, conditional on issuer uniqueness and proof |
| A replacement session inherits authority from a stale admission completion | Prohibited at lines 274-278; implementation must use the guarded commit owed by `B53`, not today's callback continuation |
| A reply selected correctly is later migrated to another writer | Prohibited at lines 313-315; compatible with existing exact writer ownership |
| The destination withdraws after admission and the receiver forwards elsewhere | The derived pin and one-hop budget address conforming AGP forwarding, lines 305-311; they do not constrain recipient application behavior |
| Missing identity metadata silently becomes unauthenticated authority | Explicitly rejected by lines 198-213 and 280-281 |
| A failure reply bypasses the restriction applied to successful replies | Explicitly prohibited at lines 355-357 and acceptance row 383; the consumer's shared reply site supports this integration |
| Lease expiry is mistaken for proof that no admission or session remains | D62's conjunctive conditions and exact handoff/lifetime facts address this, lines 125-166 |
| Authentication is mistaken for confidentiality or proof of a unique physical process | The draft separates these claims at lines 109 and 244-251 |

Possession-based credentials cannot distinguish two processes holding the same credential material.
Protection of bearer credentials and protection across TLS termination boundaries require separate assurance.
The draft's explicit limitations and carrier ownership are consistent with that distinction. [RFC 6750](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6750.html#section-5.2)

The `B6` API deferral is acceptable here.
B51 needs immutable association facts on committed controllers to enforce a send.
Production issuer reclamation additionally needs the declared pending-use transfer and exact-controller lifetime interface.
The draft neither implements nor substitutes a guessed interface for that second obligation.

## 4. Implementation guardrails

These preserve requirements already present in the draft; they are not requests for another consumer requirements round.

### Exact routing ownership

The constrained query must carry or otherwise establish exact controller ownership through declared core/node contracts.
Do not implement the guarantee by filtering an operations snapshot or resolving only `remoteNodeId` plus reusable `sessionId`.

The query must filter before canonical selection.
It must not call today's `routeToInstance()` once and reject its winner while overlooking an eligible constrained candidate.

### Guarded identity commit

Validate and snapshot the association without installing authority.
Installation belongs to the exact successful guarded commit, after expiry, terminal-state and collision checks.

`IdentityAdmissionPort.evaluate()` returning `allow` is neither that commit nor the issuer's reservation-transfer event.
B51 integration must not bypass the separately owed `B53` correction.

### Runtime value fidelity

The association and target must become AGP-owned immutable plain-data values before asynchronous work can observe later caller mutation.
Freezing only the outer object is insufficient.

ECMAScript string length counts UTF-16 units, and strings may contain unpaired surrogates.
Implementation must enforce the proposed scalar-value bound explicitly and preserve exact equality without normalization. [ECMAScript String type](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/ecmascript-data-types-and-values.html#sec-ecmascript-language-types-string-type)

`Object.freeze` fixes an object's own property attributes; it does not recursively freeze referenced objects or convert accessors into plain data.
The admission reader must satisfy the draft's plain-data and immutable-copy requirements independently of a shallow freeze. [Object.freeze](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/fundamental-objects.html#sec-object.freeze), [SetIntegrityLevel](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/abstract-operations.html#sec-setintegritylevel)

### One admission mechanism

Ordinary and restricted sends must share source ownership, size/export precedence, cancellation commit, accounting, dispositions and writer ownership.
The restriction changes candidate eligibility, not the meaning of successful admission.

After commit, cancellation or reconnection cannot move or replay the packet.
The restricted method must always derive its pin and one-hop budget.

### Account for retained metadata

The current handler ledger charges the supplied byte count at `packages/node/src/handler-ledger.ts:33`; local-delivery resolution currently supplies payload bytes.
The added association/context cost therefore needs an explicit accounting implementation, not an assumption that existing byte gauges already include it.

Copies must not retain closed controllers or carrier objects.
Application-owned retention after the handler ends remains outside AGP's budget.

### Preserve the support and ownership boundaries

Types, schemas, runtime validation, exports and structural implementers must agree.
Method presence is necessary but does not certify an adapter's behavior.

Operation-call must preserve the same captured target and protection requirement on every reply branch.
Issuer uniqueness, reclamation, bearer handling and deployment confidentiality remain their owners' acceptance obligations.

## 5. Scoped M7 mapping

| Axiom | Weight | Alignment and remaining obligation |
|---|---|---|
| A3 | Load-bearing | Credential policy stays with admission/issuer owners; routing stays in core; node enforces equality and atomic writer selection; real operation-call demand earns the public surface |
| A4 | Load-bearing | The draft preserves the lifetime finding, D61 selection and D62 obligations, and distinguishes agreement, proposal, implementation and acceptance |
| A8 | Load-bearing | Review and ratification remain separate; schema/FSM/routing/node evidence must precede consumer claims; B53 and B6 cannot close through this document |
| A9 | Supporting, scoped | Acceptance obligations cover withdrawal, replacement, stale authority, saturation and carrier differences; they are prospective sandbox obligations, not deployment conformance |
| A14 | Supporting, scoped | Unknown-option downgrade and caller-bound/host-lifetime findings become durable constraints and acceptance obligations rather than informal cautions |

No excluded product axiom is imported.
No full A9 or A14 conformance is claimed.

Layered duties remain coherent:

- Issuer and identity policy establish the association's meaning and continuity.
- Transport supplies immutable protection evidence.
- Core supplies contracts and deterministic route facts.
- Node captures authority and commits the restricted send.
- Operation-call applies the restriction consistently.
- Deployment proves carrier and issuer integration.

The principal tensions are resolved rather than hidden:

- Reconnect utility versus original-binding isolation: stable association within one allocation, different binding for later allocations.
- Unified behavior versus independent carriers: one node predicate over neutral evidence.
- Bounded retention versus arbitrarily late handlers: copied values and non-reused bindings, not retained controller history or a guessed timeout.
- Progress versus incomplete lifecycle integration: B51 review can pass while B6 production reclamation remains gated.

## 6. Deltas and closeout hooks

Required design corrections: none.\
Accepted deviations or overrides: none.

Before claiming implemented B51 behavior, the verifier must recheck:

1. Exact-owner candidate resolution and zero admission to disallowed writers.
2. Guarded association installation, including the B53 integration.
3. Immutable runtime snapshots and schema/type agreement.
4. Metadata accounting and release ownership.
5. Shared admission precedence and unchanged ordinary routing.
6. One-hop enforcement and no post-admission migration.
7. Every operation-call reply branch and unsupported-runtime refusal.

Production issuer closeout additionally requires the selected B6 producer contract and continuous reservation accounting across actual controller commit.
Browser confidentiality and operating-envelope claims retain their separate acceptance owners.
