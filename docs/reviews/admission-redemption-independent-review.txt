### 1. Previously high-priority blocking finding: repaired in this candidate’s contract

The [previous independent report](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/reviews/admission-acquisition-independent-review.txt:44), lines 44–85, identified a genuine mechanism: issuer expiry checking preceded the later node sample designated as redemption, while neither the expiry boundary nor the earlier authoritative event crossed the interface. Synchrony did not prevent the clock from advancing between those events.

The consequence was that either an expired credential could be recorded as redeemed, or the opening allowance could begin later than the event actually validated. The minimum remedy was an exact common event, sufficient declared temporal authority at the seam, and explicit refusal/registration dispositions.

The amendment supplies that remedy:

1. The issuer supplies `validBeforeMonotonicMs = E`, no later than actual credential expiry, in the agreed monotonic domain.
2. After normalization and preflight, the node takes one final sample `r`.
3. That sample must satisfy `r < E` and every active acquisition bound.
4. The inherited opening deadline is anchored to that same sample: `R = r + establishmentTimeoutMs`.
5. The issuer adopts successful recording as its redemption event and installs its prepared spent record and handle registration before another issuer decision or any handoff becomes visible.

These are explicit requirements in the [amended timing contract](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:375), lines 375–412.

My design inference is that the former permitted expiry gap no longer satisfies the contract: expiry before the authoritative sample now requires refusal. Expiry after successful `r` does not invalidate the earlier event and cannot refresh `R`. Arming uses the remaining allowance rather than another full duration.

This repair does not depend on a hidden callback or an unchecked conversion of a remote timestamp:

- The issuer selects the policy limit and validates proof, generation, unspent state and non-temporal policy.
- AGP enforces the supplied temporal guard without interpreting credentials.
- Clock identity is checked, but the text expressly does not claim that identity proves honest numeric derivation.
- Derivation from the current shared clock and issuing generation is an explicit trusted-issuer integration obligation.
- Policies whose remaining time dependence cannot be expressed as this exclusive upper bound require a reviewed extension.

That scope is stated at candidate lines 380–385 and 440–446. It is a legitimate declared trust boundary, not runtime proof of issuer correctness.

The [refusal table](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:414), lines 414–434, also closes the necessary failure cases:

- Ordinary expiry or invalid authorization on an unrecorded attempt grants no new recording or publication authority.
- Duplicate recording preserves the original record and bound.
- Arming failure cannot return successful authority; any privately installed historical fact is revoked before return.
- Exceptional issuer installation failure cannot publish an unregistered handoff or restore a genuinely spent or uncertain key to unused.
- Late issuer settlement cannot revive a released allocation or terminal use.
- Discarding prepared registration does not refund unfinished acquisition work.

Disposition: the blocking defect is repaired and independently confirmed at the static contract level. The old FAIL remains attached to hash `2a2d73991919584990df6ac57a9d3f4eb78d8f4b5c7c8fc162d4c88db61b6900`. This is not implementation-level recurrence closure.

### 2. Previously medium-priority nonblocking clarification: resolved explicitly

The previous report’s [stop-accounting concern](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/reviews/admission-acquisition-independent-review.txt:87), lines 87–104, concerned wording that could conflate ordinary stop with numerical-domain terminal freezing.

Its mechanism was interpretive ambiguity; its consequence would be premature freezing of ordinary-stop cleanup accounting or misleading resource-release claims. The minimum remedy was an explicit distinction matching B53.

The candidate now makes that distinction at [lines 582–587](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:582):

- Ordinary stopped instances can advance cleanup gauges and revisions.

This agrees with [B53’s accounting contract](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/reviews/admission-lifetime-correction-r4.md:391), lines 391–403 and 479–481.

Disposition: clarification resolved; no remaining contract-level ambiguity established on this point.

## Assessment of the two original lifecycle facts

### Per-redemption handoff or irrevocable pre-admission termination

The proposal now specifies a continuous authoritative object, not a join between snapshots or interchangeable public strings.

Reservation precedes profile work. Successful redemption is registered against that same observation before publication. The trusted reference reaches identity evaluation, and provider resolution requires both the reference and the identical registered observation. Admission is published only with guarded identity, collision ownership and association installation. A failed attempt becomes terminal only after its publication authority is irrevocably removed.

The relevant contract is in [candidate lines 73–144](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:73), 318–345, 396–404 and 626–634.

My design inference is that this supplies Zorg’s first required fact without a registration gap at controller handoff. The inherited deadline continues across handoff and admitted-but-not-established state; it does not start again at OPEN or provider evaluation.

### Exact admitted-controller liveness and terminal close

The observation is non-reassignable and identifies one attempt/controller incarnation independently of reusable session strings. Terminal state retains the admitted identity and whether establishment occurred, even if intermediate observations were missed.

Only valid terminal observations satisfy the two lifecycle-absence predicates. Unknown or incomplete information retains the lease. Issuer serialization still owns outstanding-key, current-allocation and grace checks. The proposed first-terminal-observation timestamp conservatively delays grace rather than shortening it.

These requirements are in [candidate lines 554–576](/home/apnex/taceng/agp/docs/design/admission-use-lifecycle.md:554), together with the observation semantics above.

My design inference is that this supplies the second required fact without requiring a complete management snapshot or reliable event delivery. It does not transfer lease-reclamation policy to AGP.

Thus both original facts are represented coherently in the proposed contract. Their implementation and consumer fit remain unverified.

### Layered tensions and guardrails

- At the issuer/node boundary, policy ownership and temporal enforcement are split. The resolution is an issuer-authorized exclusive bound checked at the node’s exact redemption event. Guardrail: clock-object equality must never be presented as proof of correct expiry derivation.
- At the transaction layer, synchronous issuer registration must coexist with serialized node mutation. Guardrail: implement one bounded coordinator entry; do not await the current executor while holding issuer serialization or introduce nested callbacks.
- At the lifecycle/resource layer, terminal authority can be established before physical work disappears. Guardrail: retain independent resource charges and truthful ordinary-stop versus frozen-terminal accounting.
- At the capacity layer, unknown state and unfinished work can reduce availability. Guardrail: refuse new work rather than evict live authority, refund unsettled resources or create an unbounded queue.
- At the package/consumer layer, a stronger acquisition contract costs compatibility and migration work. Guardrail: fail closed before redemption, preserve neutral transport boundaries, and keep existing caller-identity protection until verified replacement.
- At the assurance layer, a coherent same-process proposal is narrower than authenticated-browser or production readiness. Guardrail: keep those claims and gates separate.
