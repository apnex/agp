WHY THE TWO CLOSURE ITEMS PASS

1. Result application owns discovered expiry; it does not wait for timer delivery.

Failure hypothesis: a provider settles while timely, but its result reaches application after admission or OPEN expiry; an absent timer callback permits stale identity retention, collision effects, route mutation, or ACK authority, or leaves cleanup awaiting a second callback.
Disposition: rejected at design level.
R4 lines 152-179 require application-time comparison, equality-as-expired, and one serialized expiry path shared by timer and result input.
The current exact result commits the appropriate expiry in that turn, while stale owners/tokens have only disposal authority.
If preparation crosses a deadline, lines 159-161 discard both the ordinary plan and its unconsumed permit, then prepare and preflight expiry without result effects.
Lines 176-179 retain numeric-terminal replacement priority and separate work-reservation disposal from session expiry.
The later callback cannot produce another expiry, cancellation, teardown, or refund.

Failure hypothesis: delayed callback order chooses the later identity/OPEN boundary, or an early timer wake expires a still-valid admission.
Disposition: rejected at design level.
R4 lines 165-170 select the earlier elapsed absolute bound, choose OPEN on equality, use that selector for both timer inputs and result guards, and rearm positive remainders on early wakes.
An expired OPEN bound before invocation revokes the uninvoked permit without calling the provider.
After identity commit, OPEN retains distinct authority through confirmation; the consumed admission token cannot cancel it.
This preserves docs/design/fsm.md lines 173-178, 233-244, and 246-257, including confirmation with hold disabled.
It does not import a credential-redemption clock or silently redesign hold-timer precedence.

Mechanism grounding:
packages/node/src/session-controller.ts lines 571-636 currently await identity and call retainIdentity before dispatching IdentityAdmissionResolved; lines 658-715 do the analogous route wait and pending-route assignment.
packages/core/src/fsm.ts lines 371-436 and 545-629 show successful-token checks alongside unguarded admission-fault/expiry branches.
R4's action-only invocation, before-all-effects guards, complete prospective plans, and reducer defense in depth address these different ownership points, not merely the final reducer call.
The ECMAScript PerformPromiseThen operation independently distinguishes promise settlement from queued reaction execution; therefore a timely settled promise is not evidence of timely application.

2. The close setting supplies one actively enforced terminal-to-cleanup budget.

Failure hypothesis: notification write, a send ahead of it, close, or the terminal drain consumes an unbounded interval, or receives a fresh close-only allowance after earlier waiting.
Disposition: rejected at design level.
R4 lines 212-245 anchor one absolute deadline at terminal-cause commit, retain it through all owned phases, arm its wake before waiting, and recheck at phase entry.
Deadline observation commits forced-cleanup intent once, then stops the writer and directly aborts the channel outside the executor even if a preceding promise remains pending.
The guard survives close resolution until FIFO terminal observation and owned cleanup finish.
All started promises remain observed; provider settlement is not joined.

Failure hypothesis: per-send and teardown deadlines race, manufacture a second terminal cause, extend the budget, or rewrite the transport's first terminal.
Disposition: rejected at design level.
R4 lines 229-245 distinguish a write failure that can initially claim the controller latch from a later write expiry that can only force earlier cleanup.
Both-expired observations enforce cleanup once while preserving the committed cause.
A pre-existing transport terminal remains immutable even if a later abort is requested.
The separate transport contract still forbids initiating close concurrently with a send: docs/design/transport-contract.md lines 565-571.
The implementation must preserve that inherited constraint while using the independent abort guard; a notification's resolution is not by itself proof that no later send is active.

Failure hypothesis: numeric node terminalization cancels the cleanup mechanism with the ordinary runtime gate, or detachment restarts its budget.
Disposition: rejected at design level.

Lines 465-477 detach ordinary authority synchronously and run cleanup afterward without operations publication, retry, or restoration authority.
Thus cleanup is authorized separately from admission effects; closing the ordinary gate does not strand the reader.
StopPolicy.drainTimeoutMs is explicitly not repurposed as this budget or a global node-stop bound.

Failure hypothesis: a teardown deadline is presented as a proof of physical release or precise host scheduling.
Disposition: rejected at design level.
R4 lines 215, 245, 298-300, and 520-521 retain the conforming-transport/progressing-host boundary.

Failure hypothesis: disposing a continuation queue refunds provider work, discards transport evidence, or permits queued credit/hold/provider effects after fatal teardown.
Disposition: rejected at design level by R4 lines 185-210 and 247-250.

4. FIFO at result/drain joins.
Failure hypothesis: a new arrival overtakes an older queued command at a drain yield, or dequeued work moves to an unbounded executor/promise queue.
Disposition: rejected at design level by R4 lines 185-196.

Disposition: rejected at design level by R4 lines 68-84 and 509.

6. Empty route-policy batch.
Failure hypothesis: an empty policy-eligible subset skips the port or reserves no work despite the authorized admission action.

7. Finite work conservation and cancellation.
Failure hypothesis: timeout, abort notification, session removal, stop, or a settled-but-not-disposed result returns a slot and permits unlimited replacement work.
Disposition: rejected at design level by R4 lines 106-122, 126-148, 269-287, and 384-403.

A never-settling provider can exhaust its finite pool for the instance lifetime; fairness, CPU preemption, arbitrary provider-memory containment, and recovery by mere object replacement are not promised.
